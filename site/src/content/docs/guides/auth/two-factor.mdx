Two-factor authentication adds a second step after the password: a 6-digit code from an
authenticator app, or a passkey (fingerprint, face, screen lock or security key). This guide
sets both up, makes them mandatory for a role, and covers what to do when an admin loses
theirs. Strapi has no built-in equivalent.

Two-factor authentication is for admins. [End users](/guides/auth/end-users/) sign in with a
password or an OAuth provider.

## Before you start

- There is no feature to turn on: every admin can set up a second factor in their profile.
- Passkeys are bound to the address of the admin panel. Set `[server].public_url` to the
  address admins open, such as `https://cms.example.com`. Browsers only offer passkeys over
  `https://` (and on `localhost`).
- Keep `VERDIN_TOKEN_PEPPER` stable: authenticator secrets and recovery codes are protected
  with it, and changing it makes them unusable.

## Set up an authenticator app

<Steps>

1. Open your **Profile** and find **Two-factor authentication**.
2. Under **Authenticator app**, select **Set up** and confirm your password.
3. Scan the QR code with an app such as 1Password, Google Authenticator or Authy. If you
   cannot scan it, enter the setup key shown below it.
4. Enter the **6-digit code** the app shows and select **Turn on**.
5. Save the **recovery codes** Verdin shows: **Download** them or copy them, then select
   **I saved them**.

</Steps>

Codes follow the standard TOTP scheme (30-second steps, SHA-1, 6 digits). A code from the
step before or after is accepted too, to allow for clock drift. If codes are refused, check
the time on your device.

## Add a passkey

<Steps>

1. Under **Passkeys**, select **Add a passkey**.
2. Give it a **Name**, such as "MacBook Touch ID", and confirm your password.
3. Follow your browser's prompt.

</Steps>

You can register up to 20 passkeys and remove them with **Remove**. Passkeys are a second
factor after the password, not a replacement for it.

## Recovery codes

You get 10 recovery codes when you set up your first factor. Each signs you in once if you
lose your app or passkeys. The profile shows how many are left; **Generate new codes**
replaces them all (the old ones stop working).

<Aside type="caution">
The codes are shown once. Store them in a password manager or somewhere offline, not next to
the device that holds your authenticator.
</Aside>

## Sign in with a second factor

After the password, the sign-in page asks for the **Authentication code**. Choose **Use a
passkey** or **Use a recovery code** to switch method. You have 5 minutes to finish; after
that, enter your password again.

## Require two-factor authentication for a role

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings → Roles** and the role.
2. Turn on **Require two-factor authentication** and save.

</Steps>

Members without a second factor are asked to set one up when they sign in. Until they do,
they can only open their profile. In **Settings → Users**, their row shows **2FA missing**;
admins who have a second factor show **2FA**.

While your role requires a second factor, Verdin refuses to remove your last one: add a
passkey before turning off the app, or another factor before removing your only passkey.

## Reset another admin's second factor

When an admin loses their device and their recovery codes:

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings → Users** and find the admin.
2. Select **Reset two-factor** and confirm.

</Steps>

This removes their authenticator app, passkeys and recovery codes, so they sign in with their
password alone. If their role requires two-factor authentication, they must set it up again.
It needs **Manage users**; only a Super Admin can reset another Super Admin.

`verdin admin reset-password` resets the password only, not the second factor.

## Single sign-on

Admins who sign in through [single sign-on](/guides/auth/sso/) are asked for their Verdin
second factor too, unless the provider is marked **The provider enforces MFA**.

## Admin API

All routes are under `/admin/api`. The ones that change factors take your `password`.

| Route | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /auth/login` | With a second factor, answers `{ twoFactorRequired: true, twoFactorToken, methods }` instead of a session. |
| `POST /auth/login/two-factor` | `{ twoFactorToken, code }` (TOTP or recovery code), or `{ twoFactorToken, challengeToken, credential }` for a passkey. Answers the session. |
| `POST /auth/login/passkey/options` | `{ twoFactorToken }`: the passkey challenge. |
| `GET /auth/two-factor` | Your factors: `totp`, `passkeys`, `recoveryCodesLeft`, `required`. |
| `POST /auth/two-factor/totp/setup`, `/totp/enable`, `/totp/disable` | Set up (password), turn on (code) and turn off (password) the authenticator app. |
| `POST /auth/two-factor/recovery-codes` | New recovery codes. |
| `POST /auth/two-factor/passkeys/options`, `POST /auth/two-factor/passkeys`, `DELETE /auth/two-factor/passkeys/{id}` | Register and remove passkeys. |
| `DELETE /users/{id}/two-factor` | Reset another admin's factors (`users.manage`). |
